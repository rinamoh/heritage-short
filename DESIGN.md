# DESIGN.md

## Brief
- **Audience:** my family: Emaye's children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren.
- **Where they watch:** on their phones, vertically, shared through the family group chat. Many will watch with sound off at first → **burned-in subtitles** and a **9:16 primary format**.
- **Intent:** at the end they feel **grateful**, and remember that we're here because of the sacrifice of the people who came before us. Tone: emotional but restrained.

## Empathise
- **Who I listened to:** the family elders (Emaye's children), through the family group chat, on 2026-11-25. The same conversation in which they gave consent (see `ETHICS.md`).
- **What they confirmed:**
  - She came to Addis Ababa in the **1960s**, at about **16**.
  - In her area, **school didn't go beyond fifth grade**.
  - She made the journey **on foot**, alone, and **knew no one** in the city.
  - She finished middle school and started high school.
  - She was **often the only educated woman** in the groups she was part of.
  - She passed the value of education to her children, and they passed it to theirs. Today everyone in her line is educated.
  - The family calls her **Emaye**.
- **Family saying chosen for the ending:** **"የተማረ ወድቆ አይወድቅም"** (*yetemare wedko aywedkm*), roughly "an educated person may fall, but never falls for good".
- **What the story means to me:** one person's decision to take the leap alone is the reason her whole family is educated. She took the first step, then raised children who passed the same value on.
- **Insight that shaped the film:** the story isn't about her whole life; it's about **one decision**: leaving at 16. Everything after it (the city, school, children) is shown as a consequence of that moment, which is why s03 has the longest pause after its line (shared only with the ending).

## Define
- **Problem statement:** Emaye's grandchildren and great-grandchildren grew up educated in Addis without knowing what it cost her to start that. They need to *feel*, not just be told, that their education began with one girl's decision to walk away from everything she knew.
- **How might we…**
  1. …make a 16-year-old's decision feel like the centre of the film, not one event in a list?
  2. …show sixty years and three generations in 80 seconds without her face ageing on screen?
  3. …make "the only woman in the room" felt without showing a crowd?

## Ideate: options considered and decisions
| Question | Options considered | Decision | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whose voice tells it? | Her in first person / neutral narrator / a grandchild | **A grandchild, in third person** | No words put in her mouth (consent rule); speaks for the audience |
| How to keep her consistent? | Show her face throughout / never show a clear face | **Never a clear face**: from behind, silhouette, distance | Faces drift most across AI shots; ageing a face is harder still |
| How to show ageing? | Face ageing / clothing by life stage | **Three looks**: L1 her day-to-day clothes → L2 school uniform → L3 a mother's dress with a head covering | Age carried by costume, not face |
| What object stays the same? | Lamp in hand / cloth bundle | **A plain, faded greyish-black cloth bundle** she carries | One constant object across stages is a continuity anchor |
| What is the motif? | Road / book / light | **The kuraz (kerosene lamp)**: her lamp → her children's table → today's lamps | Light means education without text; AI renders light well; carries generations without faces |
| Wedding stage? | Include / leave out | **Left out** | Not about learning; adds a fourth costume to keep consistent within a 10-shot budget |
| Classroom | Crowd of students / empty room with cues | **Empty classroom**: her desk, her uniform, her bag, closed books, a map on the wall kept soft-focus in the background | Crowds and readable text are AI weak spots |
| Ending | A list of achievements / a family saying | **"የተማረ ወድቆ አይወድቅም"** + "Thank you, Emaye" | Returns to family voice; one strong line |

## Story
- **Logline:** In the 1960s, a sixteen-year-old girl walks alone from a small town in the south to Addis Ababa to keep learning, and lights the lamp her whole family still studies by.
- **Treatment:** We are only here because of her. In the 1960s, school in her small southern town ended after fifth grade, so at sixteen she wrapped her clothes in a cloth bundle and walked, alone, to Addis Ababa. In a city where she knew no one, she studied at night by a kuraz and was often the only woman in her class. She finished middle school, began high school, and taught her children that learning is worth any road. Every lamp lit in our family today was lit from hers.

Notes:
- s02 doesn't say "1960s" aloud: in Amharic a year can be heard as the Ethiopian calendar, and TTS misreads numerals. The period is carried by the pictures.
- s03: "ወደማታውቀው" (*to* a land she didn't know) is also more grammatical than "የማታውቀው".
- s07: "ማታ ማታ" was read with a stutter (repeated, then restarted), and "በኩራዝ ታጠና" read as studying *with* a kuraz rather than *helped by* its light. "በየምሽቱ" (every evening) avoids the repeated word; "ታግዛ" (helped by) fixes the meaning.
- s10: the pause after the saying uses Gemini's inline `<long pause>` tag, so the line stays one call (9 Gemini calls for the whole narration, within the 10/day limit).
- **Timing rule (v2):** each shot = 0.2 s lead-in + its narration + a 0.6 s hold, so the narration flows. Only **s03 (the decision) and s10 (the ending) hold 1.5 s**, and **s05 is 3 s of planned silence**. Total **87.88 s** with the 3 s end card (limit 60–90 s). The radio stage warns if it goes over, so any re-recorded line must be checked for length.
- Subtitle cues follow the narration segments.

## Music spotting (v2: cues follow shots; times are computed from the timeline)

The score is **one instrumental track** the length of the film (narration timeline + 3 s end card = **87.88 s**). Cue times are **not typed by hand**: the pipeline calculates each cue's in/out time from the shots it covers, so they can't go stale when shot lengths change.

| Cue | Shots | What the music does | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 "Dawn" | s01–s03 | Very sparse and warm: a single plucked string and a soft pad, no melody. Establishes the motif quietly. | Leaves room for the opening lines; the decision (s03) must feel quiet. |
| 2 "Leaving" | s04–s05 | The same texture thins out; **s05 is near-silent** (planned silence). | The cost of leaving: silence as a tool. |
| 3 "The city" | s06–s08 | A gentle pulse enters; still low under the voice. | Struggle and persistence: movement without drama. |
| 4 "Light" | s09–s10 | Fuller; the cue 1 motif returns. | Generations: the theme peaks as the lamps multiply. |
| End | end card | One sustained note fading out. | A clean ending for the AI end card. |

**ACE-Step settings (in `storyboard.json → music`):**
- **Caption:** "gentle Ethiopian-inspired instrumental, soft plucked krar-like strings, warm pad, sparse, nostalgic, slow build, motif returns at the end, no vocals"
- **Fields, not caption text:** bpm 72, key "D minor", duration = film length (computed), instrumental true.

## Test 1: radio cut

- **Date / medium:** 2026-11-26, shown to family in person / on the family group `<confirm>`.
- **Who (≥ 3):** `<fill in: e.g. father, aunt, cousin>`.
- **What they said:**
  1. **s01** sounds like a question: the end rises as if more is coming. It should be **wistful and contemplative**, and land as a statement.
  2. A **long silence after the first line**, and an **unsettling silence after each sentence**, as if the story stops where it shouldn't.
  3. Sentences are **cut off abruptly** before they finish (s03 especially).
  4. **s03:** "የማታውቀው" is pronounced unclearly, "like a child with a lisp".
  5. **s07:** "ማታ ማታ" is repeated, then the sentence restarts with "ማታ…". And "በኩራዝ … ታጠና" reads wrong: the kuraz should be **aiding** her, not something she's stuck with.
  6. **s10:** there should be a **pause after "የተማረ ወድቆ አይወድቅም"**, then "Thank you, Emaye".
- **Insights:**
  - **The biggest problem is timing, not words.** The shots were planned before the narration existed (7–10 s each), but the spoken lines are 2.6–6.9 s. The leftover 3–5 s of near-silence in every shot broke the story into pieces. This confirms the brief's advice: the narration should set shot length, not the other way round.
  - The abrupt cuts are a defect to diagnose (TTS output or assembly; see FAILURES F7).
  - Three lines need text changes (s03, s07, s10) and one needs a delivery change (s01).
- **Changes made:** narration v2 (table above); timing rule changed to narration-driven shot lengths (0.2 s lead-in, 0.6 s hold; s03 and s10 held 1.5 s; s05 3 s). After the voice switch the radio cut ran to 97.33 s, so the holds were tightened to reach 87.88 s (F10); a pause before "Thank you, Emaye"; **provider switched to Gemini TTS** with a wistful, falling-tone style instruction (fixes s01's question-like ending, and Addis AI clipped final consonants: F7).
